# Covenants & Compliance

## Springing FCCR Covenant
Fixed Charge Coverage Ratio = (EBITDA - Unfinanced Capital Expenditures - Cash Taxes Paid - Restricted Payments/Distributions) / (Scheduled Debt Service + Rent and Operating Lease Payments), tested on a trailing-twelve-month basis. In an ABL facility this covenant is dormant by default and only "springs" into effect for the period(s) during which excess availability falls below the covenant trigger (see the Borrowing Base Mechanics article for the trigger definition). Typical minimum: 1.0x-1.1x. Because the covenant is availability-driven, a borrower can avoid FCCR testing entirely for the life of the facility simply by maintaining sufficient availability — this is the defining feature that distinguishes ABL covenant packages from cash-flow term loan packages, which test leverage and coverage covenants every quarter regardless of liquidity.

## Financial Reporting Requirements
Standard ABL reporting package: monthly (or quarterly, for larger/investment-grade-adjacent borrowers) internally prepared financial statements within 20-30 days of period end; annual audited financial statements within 90-120 days of fiscal year end; a compliance certificate accompanying each periodic financial statement, calculating (or confirming the covenant is not yet triggered for) FCCR and any other maintenance covenants; and the recurring Borrowing Base Certificate on its own, typically weekly, cadence.

## Compliance Certificate Process
The compliance certificate is prepared by the borrower's CFO or controller, certifying the covenant calculation and confirming no default or event of default has occurred (or disclosing one that has). The lender's covenant agent independently re-derives the calculation from the underlying financial statements to confirm the borrower's certificate before it is accepted.

## Breach, Cure, and Default
A financial covenant breach is an event of default, but most credit agreements provide an equity cure right (a specified number of times over the facility's life, typically capped) allowing the sponsor to inject cash treated as additional EBITDA to retroactively cure the breach within a defined window (commonly 10 business days after the compliance certificate is delivered). Absent a cure, the credit moves to default remedies — which range from a reservation-of-rights letter and forbearance negotiation up to acceleration and enforcement, depending on severity and the lender's risk appetite.

## Reporting Covenant vs. Financial Covenant
A reporting covenant failure (late or incomplete delivery of financials or a BBC) is technically a default under most credit agreements but is typically addressed first through a notice-and-cure period rather than immediate escalation, distinct from the more serious financial covenant (FCCR) breach.
