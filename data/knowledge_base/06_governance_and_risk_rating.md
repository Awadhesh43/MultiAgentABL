# Governance & Risk Rating

## Risk Rating Scale
Internal risk ratings map to the standard regulatory classification categories used across the industry: **Pass** (performing within expectations), **Special Mention** (potential weakness deserving close attention, not yet a loss risk), **Substandard** (well-defined weakness that jeopardizes repayment), **Doubtful** (full collection highly questionable), and **Loss** (uncollectible, to be charged off). Ratings are reviewed at least annually and immediately upon a material trigger event.

## Watchlist Migration Triggers
Common triggers for migrating a credit onto the watchlist (typically corresponding to a Special Mention rating or the internal equivalent): sustained excess availability below the covenant trigger for multiple consecutive periods, a financial covenant breach even if subsequently cured, a material field exam exception, a significant customer concentration loss, sponsor or industry distress, or two or more consecutive late/incomplete BBC or reporting submissions.

## Delegated Authority Matrix
Every credit decision — new facility approval, renewal, waiver, amendment, overadvance approval, or watchlist migration — is approved at an authority level determined by facility size and risk rating, ranging from a single credit officer for small, low-risk actions up to full loan/credit committee for large or elevated-risk decisions. The authority matrix is the mechanism that determines which human role must sit at each lifecycle stage's approval gate.

## Three Lines of Defense
**First line** — the business and credit teams that originate, structure, and monitor the credit day to day, and own the accuracy of their own analysis and recommendations. **Second line** — independent Credit Risk, Compliance, and Model Risk Management functions that set policy, approve exceptions to it, and independently challenge first-line judgment. **Third line** — Internal Audit, which periodically tests whether the first and second lines are actually operating as designed.

## Model Risk Management Basics (SR 11-7 / OCC 2011-12)
Any analytical tool — including an AI agent — that produces an output used to inform a credit or risk decision is subject to model risk governance: it must have a documented owner and intended use, be independently validated before use and on a periodic schedule afterward, and be subject to ongoing performance monitoring against realized outcomes. An agent's recommendation carries the same governance weight as a human analyst's memo — it does not replace the human decision-maker at the approval gate, and it must be explainable and traceable back to the data and reasoning that produced it.

## Audit Trail Expectations
Every decision point in the ABL lifecycle — a recommendation, an approval, an override, or a rejection — should be reconstructable after the fact: who or what proposed it, what evidence supported it, who approved it, and when. This is a baseline expectation for both internal audit and prudential examination, independent of whether the recommendation originated from a human analyst or an AI agent.
