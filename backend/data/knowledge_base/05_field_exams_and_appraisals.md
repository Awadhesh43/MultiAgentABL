# Field Exams & Appraisals

## Purpose
A field exam independently verifies that what the borrower reports on its Borrowing Base Certificate matches reality: that the accounts receivable aging is accurate and properly classified for eligibility, that inventory exists, is properly valued, and is free of undisclosed liens, and that the borrower's internal controls are reliable enough that the lender can trust BBCs between exams.

## Frequency
Field exam cadence is risk-based, not fixed. Common triggers for more frequent exams: average excess availability below 20% of the borrowing base over the prior quarter, a risk rating of Watch or worse, a first-year credit with no exam history yet, or an industry experiencing broad stress. A well-collateralized, high-availability, strongly rated credit may be examined annually; a tighter or weaker credit may be examined semi-annually or quarterly.

## Typical Scope
AR testing: aging accuracy versus the borrower's subledger, invoice-to-shipping-document tracing for a sample of large invoices, credit memo and dispute testing to validate the dilution calculation, and concentration/eligibility recalculation. Inventory testing: sample count reconciliation to perpetual records, obsolescence and slow-moving analysis, location verification (including any third-party or consignment locations), and costing methodology review. Controls testing: segregation of duties over invoicing, credit memo approval, and inventory adjustments.

## Typical Exceptions and Their Impact
Common findings: previously unreported cross-aged or related-party receivables (immediate ineligible reclassification), obsolete inventory not written down in the borrower's records (immediate ineligible reclassification and often a going-forward reserve), unreported consignment or third-party inventory, and dilution higher than the borrower's self-reported trend (adjustment to the dilution reserve calculation going forward). Material exceptions typically trigger both an immediate borrowing base recalculation and a shortened interval before the next exam.

## NOLV Appraisals
Net Orderly Liquidation Value appraisals are performed by an independent inventory appraisal firm and set the ceiling for the inventory advance rate (see Borrowing Base Mechanics). Appraisals are refreshed on a cadence similar to field exams — commonly annually, more frequently for volatile or fashion/seasonal inventory categories where NOLV can move quickly, and immediately upon a material change in inventory mix or a risk-rating downgrade.
