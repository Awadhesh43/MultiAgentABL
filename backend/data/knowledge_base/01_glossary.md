# ABL Glossary

## Borrowing Base
The maximum amount a borrower may draw under an asset-based revolving facility at a given point in time. It is calculated by applying agreed advance rates to the value of eligible collateral (typically accounts receivable and inventory), less any reserves the lender has imposed. The borrowing base is recalculated every time the borrower submits a new Borrowing Base Certificate.

## Advance Rate
The percentage of eligible collateral value a lender is willing to lend against. Typical ranges in middle-market ABL: 80-90% of eligible accounts receivable, and 50-65% of eligible inventory (often expressed as a percentage of Net Orderly Liquidation Value rather than cost). Advance rates are set at closing based on field exam and appraisal findings and can be adjusted at renewal or upon a material change in collateral quality.

## Eligible Collateral / Ineligibles
Eligible collateral is collateral that meets the criteria set out in the credit agreement for inclusion in the borrowing base. Common ineligibles excluded from eligible accounts receivable: invoices aged more than 90 days past invoice date, "cross-aged" accounts where more than 50% of a customer's balance is past due, concentration in excess of the account debtor's permitted cap, contra accounts (customer is also a vendor), bill-and-hold invoices, foreign account debtors without credit insurance, government receivables without a proper assignment of claims, and related-party receivables.

## Availability
Borrowing base minus the outstanding loan balance and any outstanding letters of credit or reserves. This is the amount the borrower can still draw today.

## Excess Availability (EA)
Availability measured against a covenant threshold, usually expressed as the greater of a percentage of the borrowing base (e.g., 10%) or a fixed dollar floor (e.g., $2.5 million). Excess availability is the trigger mechanism for springing covenants and springing cash dominion in most modern ABL facilities.

## Dilution
The reduction in accounts receivable value caused by credits, discounts, returns, disputes, and bad-debt write-offs — as distinct from reductions caused by cash collections. Dilution is expressed as a percentage of gross sales over a trailing period (commonly trailing three or twelve months).

## Dilution Reserve
A reserve deducted from the borrowing base when dilution exceeds an agreed threshold (commonly 5%). The reserve is typically calculated as the dilution percentage in excess of the threshold, multiplied by eligible accounts receivable.

## NOLV (Net Orderly Liquidation Value)
The value an independent appraiser estimates inventory would fetch in an orderly liquidation over a reasonable sale period, net of liquidation costs. NOLV, not cost or retail value, is the standard basis for inventory advance rates in ABL. NOLV appraisals are refreshed periodically, typically annually or more frequently for higher-risk credits.

## Field Exam
An on-site or desk audit performed by the lender (or a third-party firm on the lender's behalf) to verify the accuracy of the borrower's collateral reporting — testing accounts receivable aging, inventory records, and the borrower's internal controls over both. Field exam frequency is typically tied to average excess availability and the credit's risk rating: tighter availability or a weaker rating means more frequent exams.

## Cash Dominion (Cash Control)
The lender's contractual right to sweep receipts from the borrower's lockbox or blocked account each day to pay down the revolving loan. "Full dominion" facilities sweep from day one; "springing dominion" facilities only begin sweeping once excess availability falls below the covenant trigger, and dominion springs off again once availability is cured for a specified period.

## Lockbox / Blocked Account Agreement (BAA / DACA)
An arrangement where customer payments are directed to a bank account the lender controls (a lockbox) or over which the lender has a perfected security interest and springing control rights (a Deposit Account Control Agreement / Blocked Account Agreement), enabling cash dominion when triggered.

## Fixed Charge Coverage Ratio (FCCR)
EBITDA, less unfinanced capital expenditures, cash taxes paid, and restricted payments/distributions, divided by fixed charges (scheduled debt service plus rent or lease payments). In most ABL structures FCCR is a "springing" covenant — it is not tested at all until excess availability drops below the covenant threshold, at which point it must be maintained (commonly 1.0x-1.1x) for as long as the trigger condition persists.

## Overadvance
A loan balance that exceeds the calculated borrowing base. Overadvances are only permitted where the credit agreement carves out a specific, capped, and time-limited overadvance facility (e.g., a seasonal overadvance for inventory build), and typically require heightened approval authority.

## UCC-1 Financing Statement
The public filing made with the applicable Secretary of State that perfects the lender's security interest in the pledged collateral, establishing priority against other creditors. Perfection is a condition precedent to closing in virtually every ABL facility.

## Intercreditor Agreement
An agreement among multiple lenders in a borrower's capital structure (for example, an ABL revolver and a term loan facility) establishing lien priority, standstill periods, remedies coordination, and payment waterfall in a default scenario.

## FILO (First-In, Last-Out)
A junior tranche layered within an ABL facility, secured by the same collateral but subordinated in right of payment to the senior revolver. FILO advances are the first drawn and the last repaid, carry a higher advance rate against a thinner slice of collateral value, and are priced higher than the senior tranche.

## Concentration Limit
A cap on the percentage of eligible accounts receivable — and therefore of the borrowing base — attributable to any single account debtor, protecting the lender against a customer-specific credit event materially eroding availability overnight.

## Reserves
Discretionary or formula-driven deductions from the borrowing base beyond ineligibles — for example a landlord/rent reserve (to cover a landlord's lien on inventory in leased premises), a customs or duty reserve, a bank product reserve (for cash management or hedging exposure), or a dilution reserve.

## Springing Covenant
A financial covenant, most commonly the FCCR, that only applies once a triggering condition is met — in ABL, almost always an excess-availability threshold. Outside of the trigger, the covenant is not tested at all, which is the defining structural difference between ABL and cash-flow (term loan) covenant packages.

## Borrowing Base Certificate (BBC)
The periodic report — weekly or monthly depending on the facility — in which the borrower calculates its own borrowing base and availability from its current AR aging and inventory data, and certifies the calculation's accuracy to the lender. BBCs are the primary ongoing data feed for collateral monitoring throughout the life of the facility.

## Watchlist / Criticized Assets
An internal classification applied to credits showing signs of deterioration — declining availability, covenant pressure, industry stress, or field exam exceptions — that triggers enhanced monitoring frequency and heightened reporting, consistent with regulatory classified-asset expectations (Special Mention, Substandard, Doubtful, Loss).

## Risk Rating
An internal credit grade reflecting probability of default and loss severity, assigned at origination and reviewed at least annually, or immediately upon a material trigger event (large covenant breach, field exam exception, industry shock, sponsor distress).
